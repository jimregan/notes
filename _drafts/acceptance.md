# Practice Acceptance

## Introduction

Here you'll learn what acceptance is and how it can provide an alternative to constantly fighting and trying to control things you can't. You'll identify what you should accept, and train yourself in acceptance with the help of various exercises.

The aim is to help you learn how to take an accepting approach, so that you can put up with things you can't change more easily and refocus your energy on things that are actually within your control.

---

## Fight Back

There are many things in life that aren't the way we'd like them to be. They could be specific situations, circumstances or thoughts and emotions. When our reality isn't what we want, it's easy to get caught up in fighting it. It could be that we repeatedly try to change something that can't be changed, or that we get stuck ruminating instead of taking steps forward.

---

## Acceptance – An Alternative to Fighting Back

Acceptance can sound challenging if you're struggling with something difficult. However, you should try to persevere and finish this program – it can be a groundbreaking skill.

Acceptance is about allowing thoughts and experiences to be present rather than trying to take control over, change, avoid or escape them. Acceptance is also about letting emotions be present whether they're difficult or not, and without evaluating or judging them.

---

## Acceptance is the Basis for Change

Acceptance isn't about being passive or giving up. Rather, it provides us with a much better starting point for creating change than if you're fighting back. Accepting something doesn't mean that you're happy with your situation or that you like your experiences, it just means that you accept your reality as it is. By accepting reality as it is, we can create more space and energy to change and deal with things that are actually within our control.

Let's look at an example of how a situation can be handled both with and without acceptance.

---

## Example: Eve

Eve's in a relationship conflict because her girlfriend has upset her by forgetting her birthday. She's sad, angry and has thoughts about what's happened and what'll happen in the future.

---

## Without Acceptance

Eve ruminates on what her girlfriend has done, and is caught up in worrying about the future. The rumination and worry gives her strong anxiety. She tries to console herself by eating lots of chocolate and drinking a bottle of wine. She still feels sad and anxious afterwards, and also feels guilty for drinking alcohol and comfort eating. For the next few days she feels very annoyed with her girlfriend. She has a short temper and gets angry at her over little things.

---

## With Acceptance

Eve allows her thoughts and emotions to exist, even though they feel difficult. Instead of trying to get rid of them, she leaves them as they are. She describes how she feels to herself: "I feel so disappointed and have thoughts that she doesn't like me very much." Eve reminds herself that it's human to think and feel the way she does in this situation. While she allows the discomfort to exist, she calls a friend for support and help on how to move forward. Then she takes a warm bath to take care of herself.

Later in the evening, Eve still has both the thoughts and the feelings, but they no longer control her. They're there, but now on the sideline. When the girlfriend arrives, Eve can describe her reactions and get an explanation of how the girlfriend could forget her birthday.

---

## Acceptance Gives You Room to Maneuver

All emotions come with impulses to act in a certain way. You can't choose what emotions and impulses you get, but you can choose what you do when you get them. It is common for us to let emotions control how we act without being particularly aware of it. If you pay more attention to your emotions, you will also become more aware of how they make you act. When you open up to the emotions and accept that you have them, you can more easily control your actions and choose to act in a more helpful way.

With an accepting approach, you can let unpleasant thoughts and feelings stay in the background without letting them stop you from doing what's important to you. The whole point of accepting discomfort is that it gives you more opportunities to create a meaningful life.

---

## The Beach Ball Metaphor

It can be easier to understand acceptance using a metaphor. Imagine that it is a hot summer day and that you are in cooling water. Imagine that all the thoughts and feelings that you are trying to get rid of or avoid are a beach ball that you have in the water. See in front of you how you are now trying to push the beach ball below the water surface with all your might. What happens to your attention? What consumes your energy?

---

## What Happens if You Keep the Ball Under the Surface?

The answer is probably that you direct all focus and force towards preventing the ball from rising towards the water surface. Around you, people seem to have a carefree existence where they swim, make noise in the water and enjoy the sun. At the same time, you put a lot of your energy into keeping the ball below the surface. After a while, it takes so much of your power that you have to let go and then the beach ball rebounds to the surface of the water with full force!

---

## What Happens if You Let the Ball Float?

Now imagine instead that you leave the beach ball exactly as it is, while you focus on your life and everything that is happening around you. You can still see the beach ball and it may hit you sometimes, but you do not have to put all your energy into trying to push it away from you.

## Exercise: What Is Beyond Your Control?

This exercise will help you to distinguish between things you can't control and things you can actually influence. It will allow you to focus on the things that you can change, while accepting the things you can't.

1. Pick a problem or conflict that is taking up your energy right now, something that is stressing you out at work, something that is worrying you or something that has happened.
2. Draw a large circle.
3. In the circle, write down things that you have control over. These can be things like how you treat yourself, how you talk to yourself, your boundaries, how you treat others or what you tell them.
4. Outside the circle, write down what is beyond your control. It can be things that might happen in the future, what others do and think, or what thoughts and feelings you have.

When you find yourself trying to change or take control of things that are outside the circle, try to focus on what's inside the circle instead.

## Exercise: When Are You Fighting?

Take out your paper and pencil.

1. Write down situations where you tend to fight rather than accept. Describe the thoughts and emotions you usually resist.
2. Pick at least one of the situations and write down what it would look like if you tried using acceptance instead of fighting back.
3. What concrete change can you make in situations where you usually struggle? It can be letting go of thoughts, refraining from participating in a conflict or continuing with what you had planned to do even though you don't feel like it.

## Acceptance Exercises

Let's try an exercise aimed at training yourself to accept your experiences as they are. We'll go through several techniques that can bring you closer to an accepting approach:

* stop, describe, allow, let go
* self-compassion
* consider the feeling with a child's eyes
* breathe in the feeling
* imagine the feeling as an object

Start by doing the exercises when you feel calm. Once you've practiced them a few times, you can test the techniques when you have a stronger feeling. The goal is for you to be able to use the techniques in situations where you really need them.

Please read the instructions below before performing the exercise. Then sit comfortably, close your eyes and follow the instructions. If it helps, you can record yourself reading the instructions and then do the exercise by listening to the recording.

---

## Exercise: Stop, Describe, Allow, Let Go

Stop for a moment and take notice of what you're experiencing in your mind and body. What thoughts are going through your head? What emotions are you experiencing right now? How does it feel in your body?

Observe your thoughts, emotions and physical experiences. Note and describe what's happening, but try to do so without evaluating what you experience.

Accept your experiences without judging them. Try to see if you can let your experiences be as they are, without controlling, escaping or pushing them away.

Then let go of the experiences. Make a conscious choice to move on instead of getting caught up in your experiences. Let the experiences be, and then let them fade away.

---

## Reflect After the Exercise

Reflect on the exercise once you've done it. Think about the questions below and write down your answers.

* What did you notice in your body?
* What thoughts came up?
* What feelings did you observe?
* Were you able to note the experiences without evaluating or judging them?
* Could you allow the experiences to be there without trying to change them?

Feel free to reflect in the same way after all the exercises that follow.

---

## Exercise: Self-Compassion

Put one hand on the part of your body where you feel the strongest sensation right now. Imagine that this is a healing hand—the hand of a loving partner, parent, or nurse. Spread a little warmth in this part of the body, not to get rid of the feeling, but to open up around it, make room for it and hold it. Hold it gently as if it were a crying child or a scared little puppy.

---

## Exercise: Consider the Feeling with a Child's Eyes

Notice where the feeling is in your body. Examine it carefully. Observe it like a curious child who has never seen anything like it before. Where are the edges? Where is the beginning and the end? Does it move or is it still? Is it on the surface or inside you? Hot or cold? Light or heavy? Try to curiously explore the feeling without judging or changing it.

---

## Exercise: Breathe in the Feeling

Notice where the feeling is strongest in your body. Breathe into the feeling by directing your attention to where you feel it while breathing deeply and slowly. Imagine your breath flowing into and around the sensation. Just let the feeling be as it is while you breathe and open up around it.

---

## Exercise: Imagine the Feeling as an Object

Imagine that the feeling is an object. What shape is the feeling? Is it liquid, solid or a gas? Is it still or moving? What color is it? If you could touch it, how would it feel? Hot or cold? Soft or hard? Rough or smooth?

Observe the object with curiosity, breathe into it, open up around it. You don't have to like it or want it. Just allow it.

---

## Practice Often

Practice these techniques as often as you can to approach acceptance. Then try to apply them in threat mode, that is, when an unpleasant thought or feeling appears. Try to approach negative experiences instead of fighting or running away from them.

---

## Do Not Try to Get Rid of or Change the Experience

Remember that the purpose of the exercises is not to get rid of or change the experiences. Sometimes the exercises can have that effect, but the goal is not to change or eradicate the experiences but to make peace with them and let them be, whether they are pleasant or not.

---

## Summary

* When our reality isn't what we want, it's easy to get caught up in fighting it, which often creates more suffering.
* Acceptance is about allowing thoughts and experiences to be present rather than trying to take control over, change, avoid or escape them.
* By accepting reality as it is, we can create more space and energy to change and deal with things that are actually within our control.
* You can practise adopting an accepting approach using the exercises in this program.
